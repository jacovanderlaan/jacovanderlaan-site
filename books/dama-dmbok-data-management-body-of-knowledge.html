DAMA-DMBOK: Data Management Body of Knowledge

DAMA International · 2017 · data architecture

Highlights

  • Data management is a discipline, not a tool choice — the DMBOK organizes data work into eleven knowledge areas (architecture, modeling, quality, metadata, governance, and more) that exist independently of any vendor or platform. This is the canonical statement of "platforms churn, capabilities endure": the framework describes what an organization must be able to do, and lets the tooling rotate underneath.
  • Governance sits at the center of the wheel — the famous DAMA wheel places data governance in the middle with every other knowledge area arranged around it, arguing that policies, standards, and accountability are what bind the technical disciplines together. In SBM terms this is the rules pillar made explicit: without codified rules, the other areas degrade into isolated craft.
  • Metadata is a first-class knowledge area, not documentation afterthought — the DMBOK treats metadata management as its own discipline with lifecycle, architecture, and quality concerns of its own. That is the intellectual license for MDDE's core move: if metadata deserves managed, structured treatment anyway, you might as well make it machine-readable and generate the engineering from it.
  • A shared business glossary is infrastructure — the book insists that agreed, precisely defined business terms are a prerequisite for data quality and integration, not a nice-to-have. This is a concept-library avant la lettre: shared precise vocabulary as the layer that makes everything downstream — models, rules, AI context — composable.
  • Data quality means defined, measurable rules — the DMBOK frames quality not as cleanup effort but as dimensions (completeness, consistency, validity, timeliness) operationalized through explicit rules and measurement. That is validate-don't-guess codified into a body of knowledge: plausibility checks belong in the system, not in someone's head.
  • Modeling is where business meaning gets captured durably — data modeling and design get their own knowledge area because the model is the artifact that carries business semantics across generations of systems. The model outlives the tool — the DMBOK said it institutionally before MDDE made it executable.
