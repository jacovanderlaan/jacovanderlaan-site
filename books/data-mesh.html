Why this is in my collection

From the publisher:

Many enterprises are investing in a next-generation data lake, hoping to democratize data at scale to provide business insights and ultimately make automated intelligent decisions. In this practical book, author Zhamak Dehghani reveals that, despite the time, money, and effort poured into them, data warehouses and data lakes fail when applied at the scale and speed of today's organizations. A distributed data mesh is a better choice. Highlights

  • Federated computational governance is rules-as-code at organizational scale — Dehghani's fourth principle demands that global policies be automated and embedded in the platform rather than enforced by committee; the strongest mainstream articulation of rules as the third pillar next to data and AI.
  • Data as a product means data with a contract — discoverable, addressable, trustworthy, self-describing data products are essentially data plus explicit metadata plus guarantees; a product is a dataset that has been given structure and promises.
  • Monolithic architectures fail at organizational scale, not technical scale — the book's diagnosis is that centralized teams become the bottleneck regardless of platform power; the failure mode is structural, so the fix must be structural too.
  • Domain ownership relocates modeling, it doesn't remove it — pushing data responsibility to domains means every domain now needs modeling competence; mesh quietly multiplies the demand for the skill it never names.
  • Self-serve platform = paved roads for data work — the platform's job is to make the right pattern the easy pattern, which is the generation argument in platform form: encode the pattern once, let teams stamp it out.
  • What the book underspecifies: the integration layer — mesh says little about how cross-domain history and identity get reconciled, precisely the problem ensemble/Data Vault modeling solves; the gap where this practice plugs in.
  • Vision needs an executable core to survive contact with reality — mesh adoption stalls where the four principles stay philosophy; the MDDE lesson is that operating models only stick when their patterns become tooling.
