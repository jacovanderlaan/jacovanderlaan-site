Star Schema The Complete Reference

Christopher Adamson · 2010 · data warehousing

Highlights

AI-assisted.

  • Declare the grain before anything else — Adamson makes grain the non-negotiable first act of every fact table design; it is a modeling rule in the purest sense, and getting it wrong invalidates everything generated downstream.
  • The fact-table catalog is finite — transaction, periodic snapshot, and accumulating snapshot cover essentially all measurement shapes; a closed catalog of patterns is exactly what makes dimensional marts a prime target for model-driven generation.
  • Slowly changing dimensions are enumerable responses to change — the SCD types form a decision table (what happens on change of attribute X?) that can be captured as metadata and generated, rather than hand-coded per dimension.
  • Conformed dimensions are the enterprise consistency contract — shared dimensions are what let separate stars add up to one truth; without that agreement layer, "self-service BI" fragments into silo arithmetic.
  • Bridges, hierarchies, and multi-valued cases have standard resolutions — even the awkward edge cases resolve to documented patterns, reinforcing that dimensional design is craft applied within a pattern language, not invention.
  • The star is a presentation layer, not the archive — Adamson's marts answer questions fast; keeping full auditable history is a different job (the Data Vault side of the house), and a healthy architecture generates the star from the vault rather than choosing between them.

Structure beats magic.
