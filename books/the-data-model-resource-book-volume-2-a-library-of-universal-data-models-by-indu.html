Highlights (AI · MDDE/SBM lens)

AI-distilled highlights, not personal reading highlights — the ideas in this book that connect to Jaco's MDDE / Structure-Beats-Magic work, selected and paraphrased through that lens. Paraphrased (never verbatim); verbatim quotes await the book text.

  • Industry models are overlays, not replacements — Volume 2 layers vertical-specific structures (insurance, healthcare, manufacturing, telecom, finance) on top of Volume 1's universal core, a clean separation of the generic pattern from the specific variation that mirrors how MDDE splits reusable structure from domain rules.
  • Comparing industries exposes the invariant backbone — reading the verticals side by side shows party, product, and agreement recurring everywhere while only the periphery changes; the invariants are what you can safely automate against.
  • Variation itself is patterned — the differences between industries are not arbitrary but predictable extension points, which means even the customization layer can be treated as configuration rather than freehand modeling.
  • Templates as delivery accelerators — starting an engagement from a published industry model collapses weeks of requirements archaeology into a review-and-adapt exercise, the modeling equivalent of scaffolding a pipeline instead of hand-coding it.
  • A cross-industry pattern library validates business keys — the recurring core concepts per industry are a useful sanity check when identifying hubs and their business keys in a Data Vault: if the concept appears in Silverston's library, it is probably a real enterprise anchor, not a source-system artifact.
  • Reference models teach modeling judgment — beyond reuse, studying dozens of worked models trains the eye for what good abstraction looks like; a curated corpus as a learning instrument, the same bet behind a concept library.
