Data Model Storytelling: An Agile Approach to Maximizing the Value of Data Management

Larry Burns · 2021 · data modeling

Why this is in my collection

From the publisher:

An Agile Approach to Maximizing the Value of Data Management

Highlights (AI · MDDE/SBM lens)

AI-distilled highlights, not personal reading highlights — the ideas in this book that connect to Jaco's MDDE / Structure-Beats-Magic work, selected and paraphrased through that lens. Paraphrased (never verbatim); verbatim quotes await the book text.

  • A model is a story about the business, told in structure — Burns reframes data models from technical deliverables into narratives that stakeholders can follow; the bridge discipline between modeling rigor and the communication work that decides whether models get funded at all.
  • Modeling must survive agile, and that means changing its pace — the book confronts the "no time for modeling in sprints" objection head-on: deliver model value incrementally instead of demanding big-design-up-front; MDDE's answer is sharper still — when models generate the code, modeling is sprint velocity.
  • Value first, artifact second — Burns insists data management activities justify themselves by business value, not by methodological completeness; the discipline check against modeling for modeling's sake that keeps a consulting practice honest.
  • Models as living, versioned assets rather than shelf documents — a model that stops evolving with the business becomes fiction; the same staleness rule applied to status docs in the vault applies to enterprise models.
  • The modeler's real product is shared understanding — diagrams are the medium, alignment is the deliverable; why storytelling skill belongs in the same toolkit as normalization, and why concept-library-plus-article writing is part of the same craft.
  • A defense of modeling in the era that declared it dead — Burns writes against the schema-later, model-nothing current; ammunition for the argument that skipping the model doesn't remove the structure, it just makes it implicit and unmanaged.

Structure beats magic.
