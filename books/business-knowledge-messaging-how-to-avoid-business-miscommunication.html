Business Knowledge Messaging: How to Avoid Business Miscommunication

Ronald G. Ross · 2022 · business rules requirements

Why this is in my collection

From the publisher:

The problem we face today is inelastic engagement. If you're drowning in a daily deluge of emails and virtual meetings, you know exactly what I mean. Look at it this way. Informal dialog and body language are largeley inelastic, but knowledge is growing exponentially. It doesn't take a genius to see that's a problem. This book takes business knowledge, along with data, vocabulary and rules, in an exciting new direction - business knowledge messaging. Here's how you can ... Spot ambiguity Communicate more clearly * Systemize knowledge

Highlights

AI-assisted.

  • Inelastic engagement is the bottleneck — Ross names the core problem: knowledge grows exponentially while meetings and email scale linearly with human attention; the only way out is systemized knowledge that transmits without a person in the loop.
  • Knowledge must be messaged, not just stored — a repository nobody's message reaches is dead weight; packaging knowledge into precise, deliverable units is the transmission half that most knowledge-management efforts forget — and what a folder-per-concept content system is built to do.
  • Ambiguity is detectable before it does damage — Ross treats spotting ambiguous phrasing as a learnable skill with concrete tells; the linguistic equivalent of a data-quality check, applied to sentences instead of rows.
  • Data, vocabulary, and rules travel together — the book keeps the trio inseparable: data without vocabulary is uninterpretable, vocabulary without rules is unenforceable; a compact restatement of why rules are the forgotten third pillar.
  • Written precision scales where charisma cannot — informal dialog and body language do not compound, but well-structured written knowledge does; the case for writing things down properly, made as an economics argument.
  • Systemized knowledge is a business asset, not documentation overhead — treating expressed knowledge as an operational resource with standards of quality flips it from cost center to infrastructure; the enterprise mirror of a personal second brain.
