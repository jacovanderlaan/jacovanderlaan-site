Highlights

  • 101 services is the churn layer made visible — the sheer catalog Vergadia has to illustrate is itself the MDDE argument: platform surface area explodes and mutates, so the durable investment is the model and rules that survive any service being renamed or retired.
  • One illustrated page per service — each capability gets a self-contained visual unit with its purpose, fit, and connections; folder-per-concept discipline applied to a cloud platform, and proof the atomic-unit pattern scales to reference documentation.
  • Decision trees encode selection rules — her storage and database "which one when" flowcharts turn tribal architect knowledge into explicit, followable rules; small examples of rules as first-class artifacts rather than judgment locked in heads.
  • Patterns over product pages — the book's value is composed reference architectures (streaming, analytics, ML pipelines), not feature lists; architects think in recurring patterns, and pattern density is what makes generation possible.
  • A picture aligns a team faster than prose — the whole project bets that visual references create shared understanding across engineers and stakeholders, the same bet behind explaining data architectures with diagrams before documents.
  • Managed services shift effort up the stack — as infrastructure concerns get absorbed by the platform, the differentiating work moves to modelling the domain and the rules; the cloud does the plumbing, the model remains yours.
