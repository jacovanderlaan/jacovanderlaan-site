Highlights

  • Pipelines decompose into ingest–transform–consume stages — Eagar's architecture-first framing shows the stage pattern is stable even though the AWS service behind each stage keeps changing; the durable layer is the pattern, the service catalog is churn.
  • Lake and lakehouse zones are a modeling decision — raw/curated/consumption zoning is a lightweight structural model imposed on storage, and it is that structure (not the S3 bucket) that makes the lake usable.
  • Orchestration is where the rules live — scheduling, dependencies, and quality gates in Step Functions or Glue workflows are executable rules around the data flow; the often-forgotten third pillar made concrete in cloud tooling.
  • Cataloging and permissions before consumption — Glue Catalog and Lake Formation put metadata and access rules in front of every consumer, a reminder that governance is infrastructure, not paperwork.
  • A service-by-service book ages fast; its patterns don't — reading it as a catalog of 2021 AWS services dates it, reading it as a catalog of pipeline patterns keeps it useful; a live demonstration of why MDDE bets on the model layer.
