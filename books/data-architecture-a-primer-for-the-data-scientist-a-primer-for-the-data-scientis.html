Mary Levins, W. H. Inmon, Daniel Linstedt · 2019 · data architecture

Why this is in my collection

From the publisher:

Over the past 5 years, the concept of big data has matured, data science has grown exponentially, and data architecture has become a standard part of organizational decision-making. Throughout all this change, the basic principles that shape the architecture of data have remained the same. There remains a need for people to take a look at the "bigger picture" and to understand where their data fit into the grand scheme of things. Highlights

  • Architecture principles outlive platform hype — the book's premise is that through the entire big-data wave, the fundamentals of data architecture stayed constant; direct support for the MDDE claim that model and rules are the durable layer while platforms churn.
  • See where your data fits in the bigger picture — Inmon and Linstedt argue the data scientist fails without an architectural map of the whole information landscape; the same argument for a modeled, navigable structure over ad-hoc discovery.
  • Repetitive vs non-repetitive data is the great divide — the book splits the world into highly patterned transactional data and irregular textual data, each needing different treatment; recognizing pattern density is exactly what determines what can be generated versus what needs bespoke handling.
  • Unstructured text needs disambiguation before it has value — raw text must be conditioned into contextualized, structured form before analysis; the same lesson the second-brain practice teaches: capture without structure is a landfill, not a lake.
  • The data lake without architecture becomes a swamp — pouring data in without modeling, metadata, and integration discipline destroys its value; magic (tooling) doesn't rescue missing structure.
  • With Linstedt co-authoring, the DW/Data Vault lineage is baked in — the book connects end-state architecture thinking to the integration-layer discipline Data Vault formalized, the school this shelf's practice grew from.
