Highlights

  • Data literacy is a language, and languages have grammar — Jones frames reading and speaking data as a learnable competence with vocabulary and rules, not a gift; the same structure-over-talent stance that underpins Structure Beats Magic.
  • Most people were never taught to participate in data-driven dialogue — the literacy gap excludes knowledge workers from decisions that concern them; the audience Jaco's KM/PKM training work exists to equip.
  • Knowing data types and their limits comes before charts — categorical vs quantitative, counts vs measures, and what each permits is the modeling instinct at citizen scale: understand the structure of the data before you do anything with it.
  • Every chart is an encoding with conventions — visualization works because positions, lengths, and colors follow shared rules the reader can decode; conventions of representation, not artistic magic, carry the meaning.
  • Context and lineage decide whether a number can be trusted — where data came from, how it was collected, and what it excludes matter more than the figure itself; the metadata-first reflex, taught to a general audience.
  • Fluency is tiered — reading, working with, arguing with data — a maturity ladder for individuals that mirrors how organizations mature in data management; useful scaffolding for designing training curricula.
