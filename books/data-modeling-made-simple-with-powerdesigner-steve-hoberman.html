Data Modeling Made Simple With PowerDesigner - Steve Hoberman

George McGeachie · data modeling

Highlights

AI-assisted.

  • The modeling tool as a model repository, not a drawing tool — McGeachie's PowerDesigner treatment centers on models as structured, queryable objects in a repository; the shift from diagrams-as-pictures to models-as-data is the prerequisite for everything MDDE does.
  • Conceptual → logical → physical as tool-supported transformations — PowerDesigner's model-to-model generation with link-and-sync keeps the levels connected, so a conceptual change traces down to DDL; an early industrial implementation of the derivation chain modern tools like CrossModel are rebuilding on open foundations.
  • Link-and-sync is lineage for models — knowing which physical column derives from which logical attribute, and detecting when they drift, is impact analysis at the model layer; the traceability that makes regeneration safe.
  • Metadata extensions make the tool programmable — extended attributes, custom checks, and generation templates let teams encode their own standards into the tool; house rules executed by the environment instead of enforced by review, rules-as-code a generation before dbt tests.
  • Model checking as a built-in gate — the tool's automated model-check catches structural defects (orphans, missing definitions, naming violations) before anything is generated; the model-linting idea shipping in commercial tooling since the 2000s.
  • The proprietary-tool lineage MDDE inherits and opens up — PowerDesigner (with ERwin) defined what model-driven database work looked like for two decades; its strengths and lock-in limits are the design brief for the open, model-first stack this practice bets on.

Structure beats magic.
