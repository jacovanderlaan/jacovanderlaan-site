Highlights

  • Separating storage from compute dissolved the sizing problem — Snowflake's signature architecture lets capacity scale independently per workload; the platform innovation is real, but notice what it does not touch: nothing about what the data means.
  • Elasticity removes an excuse, not a discipline — when infrastructure constraints disappear, the remaining bottleneck stands exposed: modeling, integration, and semantics; cheap compute makes bad models fail faster, not less.
  • Data sharing without copying changes the distribution game — live cross-organization access via exchanges previews the data-product era; but shared data is only as usable as the shared understanding of its keys and definitions.
  • Vendor booklets are the buzzword supply chain — a "For Dummies" special edition is marketing literature with genuine pedagogy inside; useful both for the architecture basics and as a specimen of how platform vocabulary gets manufactured and spread.
  • The pitch is always the same: this platform ends your data problems — warehouse appliances, Hadoop, and now cloud warehouses each claimed it; the durable skill is distinguishing what a platform genuinely solves (elasticity, concurrency) from what only modeling can (meaning, integration, rules).
