Highlights

  • Heroic DAX is usually a model problem in disguise — Rozema and Vlootman ground every advanced pattern in a sound fact-oriented star schema first; convoluted measures compensating for a broken model are magic covering for missing structure.
  • DAX has formal semantics, not tricks — evaluation context, context transition, and filter propagation form a rule system; once you learn the rules, "extreme" DAX becomes derivable rather than memorized — understanding over copy-paste, the book's explicit stance.
  • The relationship graph does the work — filter context flowing through model relationships is declarative logic: you state the structure once and the engine propagates it, a small-scale demonstration of why declared models beat imperative code.
  • Measures centralize business logic — one definition of a metric, reused across every report, versus per-visual recalculation sprawl; the same single-source-of-truth argument MDDE makes about transformation logic.
  • Conceptual modeling principles anchor the analytics — the authors deliberately teach business data-modeling understanding before language mechanics; a BI book that agrees the durable layer is the model, not the tool version.
  • Real business scenarios drive pattern selection — working backwards from the business problem to model-plus-measure design, rather than forward from language features; requirements-first thinking inside the analytical layer.
