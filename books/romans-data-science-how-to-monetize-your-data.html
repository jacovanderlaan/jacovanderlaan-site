Roman's Data Science How to Monetize Your Data

Roman Zykov · 2021 · data engineering

Why this is in my collection

From the publisher:

An introduction to the field of data analysis written in jargon-free language that is not bogged down by programming code and mathematical formulas. It covers the most essential topics in the fields of data science, machine learning and business intelligence that you are likely to come across on a regular basis. The main goal is to help readers get the most out of their data, make business decisions and create information products - all without paying over the odds.In a career spanning over 20 years, the author has worked as a junior data analyst, headed up the analytics division of an $10-bil

Highlights

  • Data only monetizes through decisions — Zykov keeps pulling analytics back to the business decision it should change, which is the same discipline as MDDE's insistence that the model serves consumption, not the other way around.
  • Simple methods before machine learning — the book argues most value comes from disciplined basics (clean data, clear metrics, honest A/B tests) long before advanced algorithms; that is structure-beats-magic applied to data science hype.
  • Data collection and quality precede everything — twenty years of practice condensed into the point that a mediocre model on trustworthy data beats a clever model on garbage, echoing why the durable layer is data + rules rather than the algorithm of the day.
  • Information products as an output form — treating analyses as products with users and lifecycles, not one-off reports, parallels building a content system and concept library instead of loose deliverables.
  • Hypothesis-driven experimentation as a working rhythm — formulating a testable claim before touching data is a rule-first workflow: the check is defined before the magic is allowed to run.
  • Do it without overpaying — the pragmatic tooling stance (avoid paying over the odds for platforms) supports the platform-churn thesis: capability should live in your approach, not in an expensive vendor stack.
