Data Management at Scale

Piethein Strengholt · 2020 · data architecture

Highlights

  • Scale is an organizational problem before it is a technical one — Strengholt's core argument is that centralized platforms collapse not under data volume but under coordination load, so ownership must move to domains while integration moves to standards; the same reasoning behind "platforms churn, capabilities endure" — the durable asset is the discipline, not the central system.
  • Data becomes shareable only through deliberate, read-optimized interfaces — domains shouldn't expose raw internals but stable, consumer-facing data products with explicit contracts; that is structure-beats-magic applied to distribution: the interface is a designed structure, not something a smart platform infers for you.
  • Metadata is the glue that makes a distributed landscape governable — lineage, schemas, ownership, and classifications must be captured as machine-readable metadata so the architecture can be reasoned about and automated; this is the metadata-as-code premise of MDDE, one step before the generation payoff.
  • Governance must be encoded as rules, not enforced by committee — Strengholt pushes governance-by-design, where policies on quality, security, and interoperability execute automatically at the boundaries; rules as the forgotten third pillar, promoted from review meetings into the system itself.
  • A shared vocabulary of domains and terms is the real integration layer — applying domain-driven design to data means bounded contexts, golden sources, and agreed business meaning come before any pipeline; the enterprise-scale version of a concept-library, where every named idea has one canonical home.
  • Reference architecture as repeatable blueprint, not one-off design — the book's landing-zone and distribution patterns work because the same structures recur across every domain; pattern density is what makes an architecture teachable, auditable, and eventually generatable — the model outlives whatever cloud it lands on.
