The Kimball Group Reader - Sample

Ralph Kimball, Margy Ross · 2016 · data warehousing

Highlights

  • Dimensional modeling optimizes for comprehension — facts and dimensions are designed so business users can read the schema and predict query behavior; even a Data Vault shop ultimately delivers into this presentation grammar, because the vault stores and the star communicates.
  • Declare the grain before anything else — Kimball's non-negotiable first step in fact-table design is stating exactly what one row represents; a discipline of explicit semantics that transfers to every modeling method, hubs and satellites included.
  • Conformed dimensions are negotiated meaning — the enterprise bus works only when departments agree on shared dimensions, making integration a semantic agreement before it is a technical one — the same act as aligning business keys across sources in a vault.
  • Slowly changing dimensions as an enumerated pattern catalog — Type 1/2/3 handling turns the messy question of history into a closed menu of named responses, and anything reducible to a closed menu is generatable.
  • The Reader itself is atomic knowledge — decades of Kimball Group design tips packaged as short, self-contained, individually citable articles; a curated corpus of atomic units, which is the content architecture argument in book form.
