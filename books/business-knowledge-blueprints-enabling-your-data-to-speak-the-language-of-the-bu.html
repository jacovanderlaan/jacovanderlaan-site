Business Knowledge Blueprints: Enabling Your Data to Speak the Language of the Business

Ronald G. Ross · 2020 · business rules requirements

Highlights (AI · MDDE/SBM lens)

AI-distilled highlights, not personal reading highlights — the ideas in this book that connect to Jaco's MDDE / Structure-Beats-Magic work, selected and paraphrased through that lens. Paraphrased (never verbatim); verbatim quotes await the book text.

  • Concept models are blueprints, data models are buildings — Ross separates what the business means from how systems store it, and insists the meaning layer comes first; skipping it is why so many technically correct databases answer the wrong questions.
  • Definitions are engineering artifacts — a concept earns its place through a rigorous definition anchored in the business's own language, not a developer's paraphrase; the same craft standard a concept-library holds each named concept to.
  • Vocabulary + structure = the knowledge blueprint — terms alone are a glossary; terms connected by well-formed relationships (verb concepts) become a navigable structure; Ross essentially describes a typed knowledge graph before the term was fashionable.
  • Data quality problems are usually meaning problems — when the same word carries three meanings across departments, no pipeline fix helps; semantic misalignment upstream masquerades as technical debt downstream.
  • The blueprint is the stable layer under system churn — applications and databases get replaced, but the business's core concepts persist across generations of technology; invest the modeling effort where it compounds.
  • Business rules sit on top of concept models — rules can only be expressed precisely against a vocabulary that is itself precise; Ross's stack (vocabulary → structure → rules) is the missing-third-pillar argument built from the semantics side.

Structure beats magic.
